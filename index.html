Human Story

Philosophy, Discipline & The Phone Lab

How I build as a 16-year-old student, why I reject bloated boilerplates, and how physical discipline powers my code.

📱

The Constraint of a 6-Inch Screen

I do not own a fancy desktop setup. Every single repository I run, from Crank-Nicolson differential equations to WebRTC peer meshes, is written on an Android phone using Termux.

A 6-inch screen forces extreme discipline. You cannot afford bloated 300MB node_modules directories, slow bundlers, or messy architecture. You write clean, modular, zero-dependency code that runs blisteringly fast on native ARM64.

🏋️

When Mind Doubts, The Body Acts

Being an intense thinker who reads Dostoevsky (especially Notes from Underground) comes with a dangerous trap: analytical paralysis. When you overthink, you freeze.

My antidote is raw physical discipline: lifting heavy weights in the gym, maintaining strict focus, and instantly executing. Physical grounding cuts through mental doubt. In code as in lifting: stack undeniable proof of work.

⚔️

The 4 Creeds I Live By

  • 01 Never apologize for high ambition: The world is shaped by those who refuse to be lukewarm.
  • 02 When the mind doubts, the body acts: Physical discipline kills analytical paralysis every single time.
  • 03 Master the hard things first: Differential equations, low-level compilers, and math create permanent leverage.
  • 04 Stack proof, not words: Don't talk about what you will build. Ship it and let the artifact speak.
